How Remote Computing benefits society

Authored by Ben Kangayan Linkedin Github

Introduction

Over the past few years, remote computing has changed the way we live in a fundamental way. This page serves to highlight a few ways that remote computing has benefitted society.

Remote Computing advances healthcare

From the BBC

In the past year, a surgeon in the UK operated remotely on a patient in Gibraltar, using remote robotics. This was possible due to a high speed fibre-optic connection with a delay of 0.06 seconds, with a backup 5G link and a team on standby in Gibraltar, to ensure things went smoothly. Gibraltar has one hospital, and patients often have to travel abroad (usually to the UK) to get help for more complex health issues. The realisation of this technology is beneficial in many ways:

  • Patients don't have to risk travelling while unfit to do so
  • Patients can save expensive costs to go abroad for treatment
  • Surgeons can increase their efficiency as they can help people from all over the world from one place

Remote Computing helps rural communities

The years of COVID-19 changed the way the world works - lockdown forced people to adapt and work from home, and this habit has stuck with society. Currently, about 40% of Brits work from home at least some of the time (from a Finder study). For reference, during lockdown (April 2020), 46.6% of people in employment did some work at home (ONS). While the number has come down, it hasn't reduced by much, showing how remote working has become a critical part of the working world.

This has had a sizeable impact on rural communities. Working from home mandated internet infrastructure to be built, which increased the speed of digital connection to rural areas. With this critical infrastructure in place, rural workers have the freedom to work remotely from home, saving on travel cost and time. The change enables people who struggle to commute, giving them alternatives that help them access employment.

Summary

Rapid advances in technology have enabled remote computing to advance, and a global pandemic has changed the way people perceive remote working. In tandem, these factors have allowed remote computing to become a critical aspect of modern technology development, and its benefits to society are still being realised.
